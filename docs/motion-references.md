# Motion references: what to supply, in what form, and how much

A channel's motion look is built from references the owner uploads and marks
(design: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-04-channel-motion-references-design.md`).
This is the guideline for choosing them. The short form of it sits above the
reference list on the channel's Motion tab.

## What to supply

Four to eight references per channel; twelve at most. Each teaches one thing,
and is tagged with it:

- **mechanism**: one move. A word that splits open to show a figure. A numeral
  that stays while the background changes. Bars rising as years are spoken.
- **look**: palette, type, texture, the weight and spacing of letters. Stills
  are good for look and useless for motion. The critic that reviews each
  graphic is shown the first two look references, so put the two that matter
  most first.
- **pacing**: how long things hold, how fast they leave, how much is on screen
  at once.

Have at least one of each. Up to two references may be negatives: a clip that
shows what the channel must not do, with the avoid line saying what.

## What makes a good reference

One idea per clip. Typography, shapes and charts, because that is what the
catalogue can draw: our graphics have no footage, so a reference built on
footage (masks, grain on a face, a presenter split) teaches nothing the
designer can use. A clean background. A move the owner can describe in one
sentence. Quality the channel would be proud of: the derivation copies the
standard as much as the move.

## Format

- Stills: PNG, JPEG or WebP, up to 8 MB.
- Clips: MP4, WebM or MOV, up to 100 MB and 90 seconds. 1080p or better.
- The marked window is one to four seconds. The model is shown the window,
  never the whole clip, so the window is the reference.
- No app chrome, captions, watermarks or logos in the window: crop them out in
  the marker or choose another window.
- Silent is fine. Sound is not a reference here.

## What to write for each

- A title.
- The source link and whose work it is.
- A rights note: reference only, unless the owner holds a licence.
- One carry-over line of the form "borrow X, not Y". For example: "borrow the
  one numeral held over a changing background, not this numeral or story".
- One avoid line, if anything in the window must not be copied: "not the glow".

## How much is enough

A window per reference and a sentence each. More than eight dilutes: the
derivation weighs every reference, and twelve mixed references read as no
style. Fewer than four leaves the model guessing on one of mechanism, look or
pacing.

## What the tool does with them

It reads each window into a one-page reading the owner can correct, builds the
channel's motion identity from the readings and the owner's words, derives an
exemplar per technique the channel uses, and shows those on the Exemplars tab
for approval and steering. The references' pixels never enter a film.

A reference is read only on "Save and read" or "Read", never on the upload
alone, and the reading can be edited on its card.

"Derive exemplars" on the Motion tab plans each technique from the readings,
builds it, has the critic judge and fix it round by round under a cap you
type, and stores the result as a draft version; nothing is approved for you.
Each technique on the Exemplars tab shows its approved version or the house
example, and "Refine" builds one new version from the approved one under a
plain-words steer, shown beside it with "Approve this" and "Keep the approved
one"; every version stays under "Versions" and a retired one can be restored.
"Copy motion from another channel" forks that channel's identity, references
and approved exemplars into this one.
"Propose a technique from the mechanism references" in the Derive dialogue reads
those references and, when they carry a move no technique covers, derives a
technique of this channel's own that a steer phrase reaches once it is approved.
