# Part 1: Market Analysis

## 1.1 Method and data caveats

The analysis triangulated 2025-2026 RPM data from four independent aggregators (FluxNote, OutlierKit, Frameloop, faceless.my), channel-level performance data (vidIQ, OutlierKit channel trackers, NetWorthSpot), press coverage of the AI-content wave (404 Media, Gizmodo, Tubefilter), YouTube's own policy documentation, and two large monetisation studies (AIR Media-Tech, 274 channels; Metricool, ~800k videos). No source has audited creator dashboards, so treat RPM figures as ranges with reliable *rank order* rather than precise values. The rank order is consistent across all sources: finance/legal/business at the top, entertainment at the bottom.

## 1.2 The candidate set: audio-first evergreen formats

Formats were screened on five criteria: (1) value delivered primarily through audio, (2) evergreen search demand, (3) RPM, (4) quality gap (is the space dominated by low-quality AI output a better entrant can beat?), and (5) policy/YMYL risk.

| Niche | US RPM (long-form) | Demand evidence | Competition & quality gap | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Business collapse / financial fraud documentaries** | $10-28 (typ. $18, business/finance ad rates) | How Money Works: 903k avg views/video; Economics Explained 2.3M subs; Bright Sun Films' "Bankrupt" series a proven 1M+ sub format | Quality incumbents exist but the case-study catalogue is vast; research depth is a natural moat AI slop cannot cheaply copy | Low-medium (defamation care; analytical framing avoids advice-YMYL) |

## 1.3 The pick: Business Collapse & Financial Fraud Documentaries

**Channel name: Boom & Busters** (handle `@BoomAndBusters`). The name encodes the phased scope: "Busters" carries the failure-first launch, "Boom" holds the door open for the turnaround and empire series. **Positioning: "the definitive storyteller of money's greatest dramas", launched failure-first.** Company collapses, frauds, Ponzi schemes, corporate scandals, banking crises, hyperinflations, market manias, heists and cons. Think a fusion of How Money Works' economics, Bright Sun Films' "Bankrupt" series, and MrBallen's narrative suspense, delivered as narration over maps, archival-style stills, charts and ambient B-roll.

**Scope is phased, not fixed.** The channel's destination is both failure *and* success stories (the model proven by MagnatesMedia and ColdFusion, which straddle villain stories and empire-building at scale), but the launch is deliberately failure-only: a young channel wins by teaching the algorithm and the audience one sharp promise, failure stories carry the strongest hooks for the Shorts funnel, and returning-viewer habit is built on a consistent premise. Broadening happens through the series system (§2.1) on a data-driven schedule: around month 9, introduce a **turnaround series** (Apple 1997, Lego, AMD): success stories told through the mechanics of near-failure, which widen the catalogue without diluting the tension the brand is built on. If its retention and browse performance hold up, a pure success/empire series follows. The channel name must therefore be scope-neutral (money + story), not failure-locked (money + ruin).

**Why it wins on the data:**

1. **RPM.** It monetises at business/finance ad rates ($10-28 RPM, typical $18) rather than history rates ($6-14) or story rates ($4-8), because advertisers classify the content as business/finance. Individual finance-adjacent videos are reported hitting $30-50 RPM. That is 2-3x what sleep, history or true crime pays per view.
2. **Evergreen with a deep catalogue.** Enron, Madoff, FTX, Theranos, Wirecard, Lehman, Tulip Mania, Weimar hyperinflation, Barings Bank, the Zimbabwe dollar, 1MDB, Steinhoff (a strong SA angle you know well), and hundreds more. Case studies do not age; a 2026 video on Enron performs in 2030. Search demand is persistent ("what happened to Enron", "biggest Ponzi schemes", "how FTX collapsed").
3. **Audio-first by nature.** The incumbent proof: How Money Works averages 903k views per ~14-minute video that is narration over simple motion graphics. The value is entirely in the script and voice; visuals complement.
4. **Quality gap and moat.** The AI-slop wave has flooded history, true crime and motivation, but research-heavy financial storytelling resists cheap cloning: it requires accurate figures, timelines and sourced claims. A rigorous fact-checked pipeline (which we will build) is simultaneously the differentiation and the compliance defence.
5. **Funnel fit.** Your Shorts-as-lead-magnet strategy demands story content with hooks, cliffhangers and payoffs ("this man lost $65 billion of other people's money..."). Fraud stories are engineered for this. Sleep content, by contrast, converts terribly through Shorts (the Shorts audience is not in bed hunting 3-hour videos) and punishes mid-roll ads.
6. **Ceiling.** Comparable channels demonstrate the ceiling: 100k-sub finance explainer channels earn roughly $5-10k/month from ads alone, before sponsorship (finance sponsors pay the best CPMs on YouTube).

**Runner-up / future second channel:** sleep-optimised narrative history. The demand is proven and the quality gap real, but discovery is now crowded, growth of incumbents is decelerating, nominal RPM is half, and it fits your Shorts funnel poorly. It remains a strong candidate for a second channel later because it reuses ~90% of the pipeline you are about to build (same narration-over-visuals assembly, longer renders, calmer voice preset).

## 1.4 YMYL and policy risk assessment (read before launch)

You asked for these raised explicitly. Four issues matter:

**1. YMYL exposure is real but manageable through framing.** Finance is a Your Money Your Life category. The channels that thrive facelessly (How Money Works, Economics Explained) do so by being *analytical and historical*, never advisory. Hard rules: no "you should invest/buy/sell", no ticker tips, no predictions framed as guidance. Storytelling and post-mortem analysis of documented events sits largely outside advice-YMYL scrutiny while keeping finance ad rates. Include a standard "educational content, not financial advice" line in descriptions anyway.

**2. Defamation and accuracy.** Fraud stories involve real, sometimes litigious, people. Rules: stick to adjudicated cases and the documented public record (court filings, regulator findings, major-outlet reporting); use "alleged" for anything not adjudicated; keep a source list per video (our app will enforce this). This discipline is also your quality moat.

**3. YouTube's "inauthentic content" policy (July 2025, enforcement intensified 2026).** YouTube explicitly welcomes AI-assisted channels and keeps them monetisable, but demonetises mass-produced content: templated narratives with superficial differences, generic TTS over stock footage on a fixed formula, near-identical videos at scale. Review is channel-holistic. Your defences: original researched scripts, human editing on every script, material variation in structure and visuals between videos, a consistent distinctive voice, and cited sources in descriptions. The pipeline in Part 3 builds these in as mandatory human gates.

**4. AI disclosure.** Realistic synthetic depictions of real people or events require the altered-content label. Narration in an original (or your own cloned) voice over stills and stock does not generally require it, but never clone or imitate a real person's voice, and label any AI-generated realistic imagery of real individuals. When in doubt, tick the disclosure box; it does not affect monetisation.

# Part 2: Channel Strategy

## 2.1 Content architecture

**Long-form (the product, ~98% of revenue):**

- Length: 15-25 minutes in the growth phase. This clears the 8-minute mid-roll gate with room for 2-3 mid-rolls (measured effect: roughly $7-9 RPM with no mid-rolls rising to $16-28 with 2-3, if retention holds), suits TV viewing (a growing revenue surface), and is producible at 2/week within 10 hrs of your time.
- Structure per video (retention-engineered): cold open stating the stakes in ≤15 seconds (+18% retention at the 1-minute mark), the "how we got here" build, open loops seeded early and closed late, a planned re-hook at the 55-65% mark (where the second exodus happens), payoff, then a bridge to a related video. CTAs before the final 10 seconds (only ~16% of viewers reach the end).
- Series create binge sessions and returning viewers (the key growth signal since YouTube's Feb 2026 notification changes), and they are also the broadening mechanism: launch with "Collapse" (companies), "The Con" (frauds and scams) and "Meltdown" (national/monetary crises); add "The Turnaround" (near-death comebacks) around month 9; add an empire-building success series later only if the turnaround data supports it.
- Chapters on every video (search + TV navigation), sources listed in the description.

**Shorts (the lead magnet, top of funnel):**

- ~74% of Shorts views come from non-subscribers: Shorts are your cold-audience discovery surface. But Shorts RPM is 3-14% of long-form RPM (US ~$0.33), and heavy-Shorts months measurably compress channel RPM, so Shorts exist to feed long-form, never as the product.
- Same channel, not a separate one. YouTube's systems treat Shorts and long-form separately (Shorts do not hurt long-form distribution), and the Related Video link and channel-page funnel only work same-channel.

## 2.2 The Shorts-to-long-form funnel playbook

This follows YouTube's own published conversion guidance plus the practices of the best story channels (MrBallen's short-form-first growth; Colin & Samir's data: 61% of subscriber growth from Shorts at ~2% of revenue).

1. **Format:** 45-60 second mini-stories with a genuine payoff. Completion rates peak around 76% at this length and 50-60s Shorts that hold viewers average the highest view counts (Paddy Galloway's 3.3B-view study). Do not default to 3-minute Shorts; completion rate beats duration.
2. **Two Shorts types, used deliberately:**
   - *Loop Shorts* (pure reach): a self-contained astonishing fact or micro-story, engineered to loop seamlessly. Looped Shorts averaged +143% views for Colin & Samir.
   - *Cliffhanger Shorts* (funnel): tell the setup, cut at the turn: "and that decision destroyed a 233-year-old bank. The full story is on the channel."
3. **Hook in the first 1-2 seconds**, cold-open mid-action, with a text overlay restating the hook (text overlays retain ~25% more viewers). Visual beat change every 3-7 seconds.
4. **Link mechanics on every Short:** set the Related Video link, intent-matched to the exact long-form story (not the channel generally); pinned comment with the same link; verbal CTA in the final 5 seconds. Use the "flood" pattern when pushing a hero video: point several Shorts at it in its first 10 days (83% of a video's engagement happens in the first 10 days).
5. **Pay off the click instantly:** the linked long-form must deliver on the Short's promise within the first 5-10 seconds. No slow intros, ever.
6. **Derivation, not extra production:** every Short is cut from the long-form script (our pipeline selects the 3-5 most hook-worthy segments automatically), so Shorts cost minutes, not hours.

## 2.3 Cadence (10 hrs/week)

| Phase | Long-form | Shorts | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Months 0-3 (pre-monetisation) | 1-2/week | 4-5/week | No RPM to dilute yet; maximise cold discovery and reach the YPP threshold (1k subs + 4k watch hours or 10M Shorts engaged views) |
| Months 4-6 (monetised, growth) | 2/week | 3-4/week | vidIQ's 10.2M-channel study: growth rises monotonically with upload frequency; 2/week long-form is the engine |
| Steady state | 2/week | 2-3/week | Converge toward the revenue-optimal ratio (AIR study: roughly 1 Short per 2-3 long-form uploads showed the best subscriber and revenue outcomes); keep Shorts volume in launch windows |

Your 10 hours weekly are spent almost entirely on the human gates: script editing and fact-sign-off (~3-4 hrs), voice/visual review (~2 hrs), thumbnails and packaging (~1-2 hrs), community and analytics (~1 hr). The pipeline does the rest.

## 2.4 Packaging and testing

- Thumbnails: one dominant visual subject, big short text (3-5 words), consistent series branding. Generate backgrounds with Ideogram/Flux, compose in Canva templates, human picks.
- Always run YouTube's native Test & Compare (3 variants, winner by watch-time share). Benchmarks: browse ~3.5% CTR, search ~12.5%; small channels should expect 5-8%.
- Titles: curiosity + specificity + stakes ("The $65 Billion Lie", "How One Trader Erased a 233-Year-Old Bank"). Generate 8-10 options per video, pick by instinct, refine against CTR data.

## 2.5 90-day roadmap

**Days 0-14: Foundation.**
Channel identity: name is chosen (**Boom & Busters**); immediately register the YouTube channel and claim `@BoomAndBusters`, plus the matching domain and social handles. Then the narration-voice audition (Gemini TTS voices first, ElevenLabs only if it clearly wins) and the Brand Kit (fonts, colours, look, series brands). Write the style bible (narration tone, pacing, sentence rhythm, banned clichés). Apply for the YouTube Data API compliance audit immediately (2-4 week lead time; uploads via API are private-only until it passes). Build the case backlog: 50+ stories scored by search demand and competitor coverage. Produce videos 1-2 semi-manually to validate the format before automating.

**Days 15-45: Launch and app MVP.**
Publish 2 long-form + 4-5 Shorts weekly. Build the app's highest-leverage modules first (research dossier, script studio, voice pipeline; see the spec document). Every video: Test & Compare thumbnails, Related Video links, sources in description. Watch retention curves obsessively; fix the first-minute drop before anything else.

**Days 46-90: Iterate on data.**
Double down on the series with the best retention and browse traffic. Complete the render and Shorts modules. Target: YPP eligibility around day 60-90 (2/week long-form at good packaging typically gets there; treat it as a milestone, not a promise). Begin outreach-ready media kit once past 25k subs (finance sponsors: brokerages, fintech, business tools pay top CPMs).

**KPIs by phase:** first 90 days: average view duration ≥50% on long-form, Shorts viewed-vs-swiped ≥75%, Shorts-to-long-form link CTR ~5%, subs-per-1,000-Short-views trending toward 1/80 on the best Shorts. Post-monetisation: RPM ≥$12 rising toward $18+, returning viewers growing week on week.

## 2.6 Revenue beyond AdSense (sequence, not simultaneous)

AdSense first (months 4-6), then finance/business sponsorships (best-paying sponsor category on YouTube; viable from ~25-50k subs), then affiliate placements where genuinely relevant, then optionally a podcast feed of the audio (the format is podcast-native; Fall of Civilizations built 100M+ downloads on exactly this kind of narrative) which adds a second monetisation surface at near-zero marginal cost.

# Part 3: Tooling & Workflow

## 3.1 Verdict: build the custom web app

Off-the-shelf faceless-video SaaS (AutoShorts, StoryShort, Revid, Crayo, InVideo AI, etc.) is Shorts-first and template-driven; none credibly serves 15-25+ minute narration documentaries, and template sameness is itself a monetisation risk under the inauthentic-content policy. Meanwhile your format (narration + stills/stock/charts + subtle motion + captions) is deterministic assembly, the easiest possible video type to build a pipeline for. The economics invert at your volume: the underlying APIs cost pennies while SaaS wrappers charge $30-100/month each for worse control.

**Decision: build the Boom-Busters production app (repo/infra naming: `boom-busters`), a single-user app orchestrating the APIs below, with human approval gates at the points where quality is actually created.** Full spec and development plan in the companion document.

Rule of thumb: tooling spend stays under 20% of trailing-month revenue, with the lean floor always available to fall back to.

## 3.3 Interim workflow (weeks 1-6, while the app is built)

1. Research: Claude (chat) builds a cited fact dossier per case; you verify the key claims (30-45 min).
2. Script: Claude drafts chapter by chapter against the dossier and style bible; you edit in a doc (60-90 min). This gate never disappears.
3. Voice: run chapters through the chosen provider (Gemini 3.1 Flash TTS via AI Studio, or ElevenLabs Studio at stability ~35-40%); regenerate flawed paragraphs (20-30 min).
4. Visuals: shot list from Claude; pull Pexels/Pixabay clips and generate Flux stills; assemble in CapCut or DaVinci Resolve with slow zooms (2-3 hrs, the bottleneck the app eliminates).
5. Shorts: pick 3-4 script moments, cut verticals with karaoke captions in CapCut (45 min).
6. Package and publish: Canva thumbnail x3 variants, Test & Compare, chapters, sources, Related Video links on Shorts.

The app collapses steps 3-5 from ~4 hours to ~30 minutes of review.
